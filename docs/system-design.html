How Bento works

Bento is a team of small programs, like a restaurant kitchen where each cook has one job and they pass order slips along a rail. Pick a story below and watch a request travel through the system, one step at a time, explained in plain words.

    People and outside servicesYou, GitHub (where code lives), your live website and Gmail (sends the emails).
    The front doorCaddy is the only part open to the internet. It makes the connection secure (the 🔒) and sends people inside.
    The head waiterThe API gateway takes every request, checks who you are and hands jobs to the kitchen.
    The order railRedis Streams: job slips wait here in line. If a cook crashes, another picks up the slip 30 seconds later, so nothing is lost.
    The cooksSeven workers, each with one job: scheduling, visiting sites, spotting problems, reading code, advising, fixing and messaging.
    The filing cabinetsPostgres keeps the records, TimescaleDB keeps every site visit over time, and Ollama is the small AI model that runs on Bento's own server.
    Bento runs as 14 containers on one server, started together with Docker Compose. Live at bento.104-43-56-1.sslip.io. The plain-language guide is in how-bento-works.md.
